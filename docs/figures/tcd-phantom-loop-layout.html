TCD Phantom Loop Layout

Two drawings. The $100 bench rig that gets built first, and the real loop it turns into once Group 1 casts a wall-less vessel. The second is not a bigger copy of the first, and the differences are where the design decisions sit.

1  Phase 0 bench rig

Built from parts on hand. Decides the architecture before anything expensive is bought.

  • fluid
  • signal
  • power

Drawing scrolls sideways.

  • The pump is the device under test, not just the drive. Ripple frequency is rollers × RPM / 60 and it tracks pump speed, never commanded heart rate. E3 is the experiment that decides whether a peristaltic belongs in the final build.
  • Motor on its own rail, grounds tied at one point. The pressure sensor's output is a fixed fraction of its supply, so slow supply drift cancels when the ADC shares that 5 V reference. Fast motor transients do not cancel. Keep them apart.
  • The standpipe does three jobs. An open vertical tube cannot exceed its own height, so it is the pressure ceiling. It reads mean pressure better than a ±9 mmHg transducer. And it is a primary standard, which is what you calibrate the transducers against. It will not give you a waveform.
  • One host owns both streams. Doppler audio and pressure land on the same machine so they share a clock. Harmless for Phase 0, since PI is a ratio and the compliance sweep compares cycle averages, but it costs nothing now and saves a rebuild if anyone later wants pressure-to-velocity phase.
  • Commanded, actual, measured are three different numbers. Calibration compares measured against actual, never against commanded. That is why the scale is in the drawing.

2  The real loop, and the seam between the teams

Same topology, different vessel. Group 1 casts a wall-less 3 mm lumen, which changes where the plumbing has to taper.

  • The fitting is the only item with a hard ordering constraint. You cannot add a fitting to a wall-less lumen after the fact. It has to be buried in the block while it is cast, which means Group 1 cannot freeze the mold until Group 2 picks the hardware.
  • Pressure side only. A wall-less lumen has no wall. Nothing resists collapse but the gel, so the block goes downstream of the pump with an open reservoir after it, and never on the suction side.
  • The ceiling has to be mechanical, not procedural. The pump will deliver its full 20 psi, about 1000 mmHg, against a kink. That is roughly ten times what the phantom needs and well past what soft gel holds. One kinked line destroys days of casting.
  • The vessel is a compliance neither team controls. With no wall it is the softest element in the loop and it dilates every beat, so 3 mm is a time-varying number. Any velocity computed as flow over a fixed area carries that error. Tune with the real block in the circuit, or with a compliance-matched stand-in.
